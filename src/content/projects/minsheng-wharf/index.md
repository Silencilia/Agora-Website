Minsheng Wharf is located in the northern part of Pudong, Shanghai, on the south bank of Huangpu River. The site is within 4 kilometres of the core area of Lujiazui, outlooking Yangpu Industrial Zone across the Huangpu River with a shoreline stretching approximately 740 metres.

Minsheng Wharf was established in the early 1900s and used to be a industrial wharf for loading bulk grain and sugar in Shanghai Port. It currently contains more than ten functional industrial warehouses built in different periods, from the Neo-Classicist redbrick warehouses by early western colonizers to the PRC-built industrial landmarks of 80,000-ton and 40,000-ton silos on the east side, the largest grain silos in Asia. As an important witness to Shanghai’s port prosperity and industrial development, Minsheng Wharf carries unique historical value, and is at the threshold of a brand new urban transformation.

DCA Shanghai was invited to a competition for the redevelopment masterplan of Minsheng Wharf. Rather than replacing the industrial fabric, the proposal preserves it and works through subtraction and addition, easing the inhuman scale of the plant and knitting the site back into the city with a network of streets and plazas that runs from the avenue to the river. Programme is matched to the spaces that suit it: the silos become a museum and art depot, the red-brick warehouses hold hotel and offices, and the long-span riverfront warehouses open up to retail and recreation. Along the water, the flood barrier that once sealed off the site gives way to a cascading landscape, while the wharf’s old network of cargo corridors and conveyor ramps is reinhabited as an industrial heritage visit route that leads from the ferry barge, through the warehouses, to the museum inside the silos.
