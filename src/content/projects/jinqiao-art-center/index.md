      The interior spaces are organized in several independent clusters, where ticketing are set at entrances into each individual venues.

      When the atrium is opened, the public spaces of the art center are shared with the cities, and visitors are allowed to freely roam around while each gallery cluster is run by an individual operator. When the atrium enclosure is closed, the Art Center functions as a traditional monolithic musuem, while the perimeter community spaces remain leasible to external agencies.

      The roof falls upon thirty-two 18mx18m structral modules. The roof form initiates from flat plates. To allow for natural lighting, one side of the roof is lifted to create a shingled configuration. Upon further structural studies, a curved profile is selected for the roof section, as this minimizes the structural height with an efficient combination of internal force flow: tensile hanging forces carried through the axial beams and bending-moments taken by the curved truss structure. Lateral forces within each module is minimized with this setup, effectively eliminating the need for lateral bracings to the columns.

Jingqiao Central Park development is an anchor development in the uprising of Jingqiao as a new epicenter of Shanghai. Six renowned international architectural offices were invited to compete for the three key buildings in the development plan, among which is the Jingqiao Art Center.

Given the vastly ambiguous nature of the client program brief and the Central Park’s explicit civic quality, DCA envisioned an art center parting way with the collection-centered classic museum model, replacing it with a social condenser that lends itself as a gathering place for the city and challenges the traditional cultural programs with an fusion with community programs. The final project took form as an stack of said programs under an all-encompassing roof permeated with light, air, and nature.
