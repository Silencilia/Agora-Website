The site sits in West Kowloon, a district projected to become one of Hong Kong's emerging city centres, and was first conceived together with the adjacent XRL (Express Rail Link) station as part of a larger topside development plan. It lies on the continuum of the West Kowloon Parkway, the chain of urban green spaces running across the district to the waterfront. Planning approval issues suspended the original mixed-use scheme after the station and the site's own basement had already been built, so the new design had to work with a structure that was fixed and make itself an integral part of the Parkway sequence. KPF was invited by the owner, SHKP, into a competition to reconceive the site, and after being awarded the project continued with SHKP to develop the scheme for Town Planning Board submission. I designed the client-selected competition scheme and was the main contributor to the office towers in the submission phase, and also took part in the retail podium facade design.

The programme's demands are unusual. Hong Kong's principal pedestrian movement arrives at L2 through the Parkway's footbridge network, and vehicles reach the drop-off on the same level, while the client's base scheme of three towers arranged longitudinally along the site cut the office drop-off into separate zones disconnected from the city — leaving no coherent sense of arrival. Our counter-proposal deforms the three tower volumes and revolves them around a single concentrated drop-off plaza; the driveway ramps carry on up to L3 so that the whole of L2 can be given to retail. The result is a shared plaza with a memorable office arrival, and a set of dynamic open spaces that activate the retail below.

Because the basement was already in the ground, the towers had to accept its structure: a transfer plate below L2 transitions the podium column grid into the tower grids, and transfer beams were added beneath the terrace floors to reconcile the shifts where the plan changes. The cascading form proved demanding structurally, and 3D structural modelling was used to expose and resolve the resulting complexities — particularly around the driveway between the L3 drop-off and the retrofitted underground parking. Above, the towers' overhang traces a retail colonnade along the site boundary while their terraces cascade toward the landscape, developed with the landscape architect JCFO so that the outdoor ground and the indoor public rooms read as one urban experience and close the missing link between the upstream Parkway and the XRL Crossing.
